## 2. Comparability notes
- Consumption: Fingrid and Elering both = gross national consumption incl. losses. Konkurentsiamet's 7,031 GWh (E9) is network-delivered consumption – do not mix.
- Peak: Fingrid 15-min averages; Energiateollisuus figure differs (13,681 vs 13,633 MW) – pick one source and label it. Estonia's 1,467 MW is Elering 15-min/hourly; comparable order.
- Forecasts: Fingrid gives ranges across scenarios; Elering gives one trajectory per report (two reports disagree by 0.4–1 TWh). Elering peaks are cold-winter vs average-winter; Fingrid publishes no peak forecast.
- Wind/solar: FI wind from the industry association (nominal); EE from Elering's net installed capacity. EE solar is "connected" incl. distribution; FI solar total is not published by Fingrid.
- Storage: FI ~1,050 MW = all reported storage ≥1 MW + estimate of small; EE has no single Elering total – the 27 MW VKA figure is reserve-participating grid-scale only, Elektrilevi's 74 MW is distribution only, Hertz 1+2 (200 MW) came online in 2026. Same-definition total for EE does not exist.
- Reserves: FI figures are national obligations; EE "prequalified" is supply, not demand; only mFRR-up demand (600 MW) is stated. Price comparison is Elering's own table (€/MW/h, same unit both countries).
- Grid: both TSOs count their own lines; EE 330 kV ≈ FI 400 kV role; EE's 110 kV is largely TSO-owned, FI's 110 kV partly DSO-owned.

## 3. Map-ready facts (one sentence each)
Finland
1. Finland used 84.6 TWh of electricity in 2025, +1.9 % (Fingrid, 4.3.2026, F1).
2. Fingrid expects consumption to rise from under 90 TWh to 106–123 TWh by 2030 and 110–162 TWh by 2035 (30.9.2026, F5).
3. Consumption record 15,553 MW on 8.1.2026 17:00–17:15 (Fingrid, F1).
4. Wind capacity 9,433 MW at end 2025, +1,023 MW in the year (Suomen Uusiutuvat, 31.12.2025, F6).
5. 1,509 MW of wind and solar connected in 2025: 1,106 MW wind, 403 MW solar (Fingrid annual report, F4).
6. About 1,050 MW of storage connected; 85 % of units under 2 MW, 56 % in distribution grids (Fingrid, 24.2.2026, F13).
7. Storage connection agreements over 4 GW, data centres nearly 5 GW, electric boilers over 3 GW (Fingrid, 18.8.2026, F14).
8. Grid: 5,400 km 400 kV, 1,000 km 220 kV, 7,600 km 110 kV, 138 substations (Fingrid, 2025, F12).
9. Links: Sweden AC 1,500 MW import + Fenno-Skan 1,200 MW, Norway ~120 MW, Estonia 1,016 MW (Fingrid, 11.8.2025, F9).
10. 2026 reserve obligations: FCR-N 128 MW, FCR-D up 309 / down 298 MW, aFRR ~50 MW, mFRR up 880–1,300 MW (Fingrid, F16).

Estonia
1. Estonia consumed 8.3 TWh in 2025, +1 %; 63 % produced domestically, 37 % imported (Elering via ERR, 26.1.2026, E1).
2. Peak 1,467 MW on 19.2.2025; record 1,595 MW on 4.1.2024 (Elering, E4/E5).
3. Elering forecasts 10.4 TWh by 2030 and 12.2 TWh by 2035, peak up to 2,246 MW in a cold winter (VKA, 9.12.2025, E4); arengukava 7/2026: 10.0 / 11.2 TWh, peak 1,924 / 2,148 MW (E5).
4. Wind 694 MW and solar 1,214 MW installed in 2025 (Elering VKA, Tabel 2.6, E4).
5. Renewables were 68 % of domestic production and 41 % of consumption in 2025 (Elering via ERR, E1).
6. Hertz 1 Kiisa (100 MW) and Hertz 2 Aruküla (100 MW/200 MWh) form a 200 MW/400 MWh complex, €170 M (ERR 3.2.2026 and 7.7.2026, E11/E12).
7. Frequency reserves cost 74 M€ in Feb–Dec 2025; 2026 forecast 60 M€, 3.73 €/MWh on bills (Elering, 2/2026, E16).
8. Estonia's mFRR-up capacity price fell from 43.90 €/MW/h (2025 avg) to 8.46 €/MW/h in April 2026 (Elering, 5/2026, E13).
9. Grid: 1,851 km 330 kV, 3,342 km 110 kV, 158 substations; import capability 2,165 MW (Elering arengukava, 9.7.2026, E5).
10. EstLink 3 (700 MW) is now planned for 2038–2040 (Elering arengukava, E5; Fingrid, 6.2.2026, F11).

## 4. Striking comparisons
1. Scale ≈ 10:1 (84.6 vs 8.3 TWh; 15.6 GW vs 1.5 GW peak) but Finland's forecast growth to 2035 (+25–77 TWh) is three to nine times Estonia's entire system.
2. Storage: Finland ~1,050 MW connected and >4 GW contracted; Estonia's grid-scale fleet went from 27 MW (2025) to ~300 MW with Hertz 1+2 — yet Estonia's mFRR-up price (18.78 €/MW/h, Jan–Apr 2026) was ~9× Finland's (1.98) in Elering's own comparison.
3. Estonia imports 37 % of its electricity; a single cable (EstLink 2) outage cut FI–EE capacity by 65 % for six months in 2025; the next link is 12+ years away.
4. Finland's connection pipeline (data centres ~5 GW, storage >4 GW, boilers >3 GW) exceeds Estonia's total installed capacity (3,687 MW) several times over.

## Discrepancies and the choice made for the site (2.11.2026)
- FI 2025 consumption 84.6 (Fingrid) vs 84.5 (Fingrid Q3 table) vs 85 (ET) → site uses 84.6 TWh, Fingrid 4.3.2026.
- FI 2025 peak 13,633 vs 13,681 MW → site uses the all-time record 15,553 MW, 8.1.2026 (Fingrid), labelled as such.
- EE 2030/2035 forecast: VKA 12/2025 (10.4 / 12.2 TWh) vs arengukava 7/2026 (10.0 / 11.2 TWh) → site uses the newer arengukava 7/2026, labelled.
- Storage grid-fee exemption: ELTS § 71 lg 10¹ in force 1.1.2026; the 14.2.2026 act extended the same conditions to the island-operation fee (§ 42⁷ lg 8). Elering's 2027 tariff raises storage costs; Elering opposes a battery discount (ERR 23.10.2026).
- Fingrid's 2026 reserve obligations supersede the 2024 figures used on the site so far.
